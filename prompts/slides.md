## This run produces a presentation

The output is a **slide deck** (PowerPoint), generated by code from the
document you help produce. A presenter talks over it; an executive reads it
without the presenter. Each section becomes one or more slides, each exhibit
gets a slide of its own, and the full prose goes into the speaker notes.
Evidence rules are unchanged: every claim still keeps its source.
{{if eq .Role "planner"}}
### Planning a deck

- `report_type`: "Briefing", "Strategy Deck", "Market Overview", "Technical
  Overview" or similar.
- `report_shape`: a presentation — the answer first, then 4 to 7 sections
  that each make one point, then implications and next steps.
- `research_questions`: favour questions whose answers are a number, a
  ranking, a comparison or a sequence, because those become slides.
- `deliverables`: 6 to 10 exhibits; at least half charts.
{{end}}
{{if eq .Role "synthesizer"}}
### Synthesising for a deck

Give every outline section a `purpose` that is the single point the section
makes, written as a full sentence a presenter could say aloud. List at least
one visual per section where the evidence supports one.
{{end}}
{{if eq .Role "editorial"}}
### Writing for slides

- Every section has a `takeaway`: one declarative sentence of at most 18
  words that states the section's point and can stand as a slide headline
  ("Unit cost fell 26% in two years while release frequency quadrupled"),
  not a topic label ("Cost and delivery").
- Write 4 to 7 sections. Keep each to 2 to 4 short paragraphs: the **first
  sentence of each paragraph is shown as a bullet on the slide**, so it must
  carry the point with its number and stand on its own; the rest of the
  paragraph is what the presenter says and is kept in the notes.
- Give each section at least one exhibit, and put the paragraph that
  interprets an exhibit directly before or after it: that paragraph's first
  sentence is shown beside the exhibit.
- Prefer charts to tables. Keep tables to at most 6 rows and 5 columns, with
  short cells; split a larger table in two.
- Provide 3 or 4 headline `metrics` for the whole deck.
- `key_findings`: 3 to 6, each `headline` at most 14 words.
- `conclusion`: 2 to 4 paragraphs whose first sentences are the next steps
  or implications, most important first.
- Use `layout: full`.
{{end}}
